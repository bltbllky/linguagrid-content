## Patterns
1. **Bracket only on one side (4 rows).** One word of a pair has a bracket and the other has the bare word, so the bare prompt
   also fits the bracketed word:
   - leven "to live" fits wonen "to live (somewhere)";
   - weten "to know" fits kennen "to know (someone)";
   - leraar "teacher" fits lerares "teacher (female)";
   - rijden "to drive" fits autorijden "to drive a car".

   Suggestion: bracket both sides ("to live (be alive)", "to know (a fact)", "teacher (male)", "to ride").
2. **Adverb sense vs adjective sense (2 rows).** heel and erg are met at A1 mostly as "very"; the prompts use the adjective
   sense ("whole", "awful").
3. **Sense differs from Turkish or the A1 sense (3 rows).**
   - kaart "map": Turkish gives card and map;
   - poetsen "to polish": Turkish gives "to brush";
   - vriendin "girlfriend": the main sense is "female friend".
4. **English above A1 (2 rows).**
   - woning "dwelling";
   - nerveus "tense": "nervous" is the direct sense, kept apart from zenuwachtig (A2) by level.
5. **Dutch lemma casing (2 rows).** engels and nederlands should be Engels and Nederlands in Dutch spelling. Check that
   lowercase is not a pipeline rule first.
6. **Style (2 rows).**
   - trouwen: "to get married";
   - rechts: "right (direction)".
